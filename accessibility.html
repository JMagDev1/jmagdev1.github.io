Accessibility

I want everyone to be able to explore my projects, however they use the web. This site aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

Your device settings come first

The site follows the settings you've already chosen on your computer or phone. It never overrides them on its own.

  • Light or dark mode: the site matches your device until you pick a theme yourself, and you can switch back to "Match my device" at any time.
  • Reduce motion: if your device asks for less motion, the moving circuit board, typing text, spinning skills and other animations stay still.
  • High contrast and contrast themes (for example Windows contrast themes): your colours are used, and selected buttons, progress bars and the current image stay clearly marked.
  • More contrast and reduced transparency: borders and secondary text get stronger, and see-through panels become solid.
  • Text size and zoom: text follows your browser's font size, and pages reflow to a single column without sideways scrolling up to 400% zoom.

Accessibility & display settings

Use the button with the person icon at the top of every page to:

  • choose a colour theme: Match my device, Light or Dark
  • reduce animations on this site, even if your device doesn't ask for it
  • turn on Easier reading, which uses the Atkinson Hyperlegible font (designed for low-vision readers), adds more space between lines and words, and underlines every link
  • have the page read aloud
  • reset everything back to your device settings

These choices are only saved in your browser on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Read aloud

Press Listen to this page on any project, or Read this page aloud in the settings. The site uses your device's own voice and speed. To hear just part of a page, select the text first.

While it's reading, a small bar at the bottom of the screen lets you pause, skip back or forward a paragraph, change the speed, or stop. The part being read is highlighted. It never starts by itself, and if you use a screen reader you won't need it.

Using a keyboard

  • Tab moves through links and buttons in reading order, and a visible outline always shows where you are.
  • The first Tab on each page offers Skip to content.
  • Ctrl + K (or ⌘ + K on a Mac) opens search. Use the arrow keys to choose a result and Enter to open it. This is the only shortcut, and there are no single-key shortcuts.
  • Esc closes search, the image viewer, menus, tooltips and the settings panel, and returns you to where you were.
  • In the image viewer, the left and right arrow keys move between pictures.
  • The project timeline can be scrolled with the Earlier and Later buttons, the arrow keys, or by tabbing through each project.

Screen readers

Pages use proper headings, landmarks, lists and tables, so they work with screen readers such as NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, TalkBack and Narrator. Filters say how many projects are showing, buttons say whether they're on or off, and links that open a new tab say so.

Images and video

Every project image has a written description. The game video on the Nature Explorer page has no sound and comes with a written description of what happens in it. Nothing plays automatically.

Known limitations

  • The two GCSE coursework PDFs were made for school and may not work fully with screen readers. The main points are covered on the matching project pages.
  • The timeline on the home page is a visual overview. The same information is in the table view on the All projects page.

Found a problem?
