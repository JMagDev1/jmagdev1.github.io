About This Project

Outrider is a complete RC vehicle system that I'm building from the ground up, covering the firmware, the electronics, the physical design and the branding.

An ESP32 connects to an Xbox Series X controller over Bluetooth, drives the motor through a BTS7960 motor driver and steers with a servo. Motor control, steering and the lighting system are all working.

The project isn't complete yet, but it will be soon. This page will be updated as the final build comes together.

Key Features

  • ESP32 firmware that pairs with an Xbox Series X controller over Bluetooth
  • Motor control through a BTS7960 driver using ESP32-native LEDC PWM
  • Servo steering with a tuned joystick deadzone
  • Lighting system with headlights, hazards and turn indicators on the controller bumpers
  • Custom 190 × 140 mm controller PCB, fabricated by PCBWay
  • High-current external power switch: through-hole pads wired to a panel-mount slide switch on the +12 V rail
  • Parametric OpenSCAD tube-frame buggy chassis
  • Overlander-style body concept and "Outrider" stickers for the PCB panels

Challenges & Solutions

The BTS7960 library I started with only supported AVR boards, so I replaced it with ESP32-native LEDC PWM functions.

The steering initially went the wrong way. The Xbox controller library was returning joystick values as unsigned numbers instead of signed ones, so I fixed the conversion and corrected the steering direction. I also had to resolve GPIO pin conflicts on the ESP32.

Before ordering the PCB I reviewed the Gerber files and found an empty Edge_Cuts layer, which I fixed before submitting the order to PCBWay.

Chassis and body

I analysed an existing 1/10 scale RC truck chassis (about 294 mm wheelbase) from its STL file, then generated a parametric tube-frame buggy chassis in OpenSCAD with rendered previews. The body is an overlander-style concept, drawn in SVG.

Tuning notes

Confirmed working values in the firmware: joyDeadzone = 200, centerPulse = 1200, maxOffset = 650, with the steering servo on GPIO 19.
